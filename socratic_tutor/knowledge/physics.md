# topic: equations_of_motion

For an object moving with constant acceleration, three key relationships connect velocity, displacement, and time. The first equation relates final velocity to initial velocity, acceleration, and time: v = u + at, where u is initial velocity, v is final velocity, a is acceleration, and t is time elapsed.

The second equation gives displacement in terms of initial velocity, time, and acceleration: s = ut + (1/2)at^2. This is derived by integrating velocity with respect to time under constant acceleration.

The third equation eliminates time and relates velocity directly to displacement: v^2 = u^2 + 2as. This is useful when the time of motion is not known or not required.

These three equations only hold under the assumption of constant acceleration. If acceleration varies with time, calculus-based methods (integration of a(t)) must be used instead.
