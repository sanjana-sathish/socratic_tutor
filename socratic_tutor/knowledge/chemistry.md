topic: chemical_bonding

An ionic bond forms through the complete transfer of one or more electrons from a metal atom to a non-metal atom, producing oppositely charged ions held together by electrostatic attraction. Sodium chloride is the standard example: sodium loses one electron to become Na+, chlorine gains it to become Cl-.

A covalent bond forms through the sharing of electron pairs between two atoms, typically between non-metals. A single covalent bond shares one pair, a double bond shares two pairs, and a triple bond shares three pairs.

Bond strength and length are inversely related: triple bonds are shorter and stronger than double bonds, which are shorter and stronger than single bonds, for a given pair of atoms.
