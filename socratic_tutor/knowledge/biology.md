topic: cell_respiration

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells break down glucose in the presence of oxygen to release energy stored as ATP. The overall reaction is glucose plus oxygen yielding carbon dioxide, water, and energy.

The process occurs in three main stages: glycolysis in the cytoplasm, the Krebs cycle in the mitochondrial matrix, and the electron transport chain across the inner mitochondrial membrane, which produces the majority of the ATP yield.

In the absence of oxygen, cells instead perform fermentation, producing lactic acid (in muscle cells) or ethanol and carbon dioxide (in yeast), yielding far less ATP per glucose molecule than aerobic respiration.
