topic: derivatives

The derivative of a function measures its instantaneous rate of change at a point, defined as the limit of the average rate of change as the interval shrinks to zero: f'(x) = lim(h to 0) of [f(x+h) - f(x)] / h.

The power rule states that the derivative of x^n is n times x^(n-1), for any real exponent n. This is one of the most frequently applied differentiation rules.

The chain rule handles composite functions: the derivative of f(g(x)) is f'(g(x)) times g'(x). This is essential whenever one function is nested inside another.
